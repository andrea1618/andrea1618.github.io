Measuring Provincial Bond Risk Premia in China

Zheyu Yang · Working Paper · 2024

How much are investors compensated for holding the debt of one Chinese province rather than another? This paper develops a framework for measuring provincial bond risk premia in China and studies what those premia reveal about the pricing of regional risk. Provincial bonds sit at the intersection of sovereign credit, local public finance, and regional development policy. They are often treated as relatively safe assets, yet their pricing can still reflect differences in fiscal capacity, growth prospects, debt burdens, industrial exposure, land and housing markets, and expectations of policy support.

The project asks whether China's bond market distinguishes meaningfully across provinces, and if so, which regional characteristics matter most. Rather than treating provincial yields as a single national curve, the paper separates common movements in interest rates from province-specific compensation for risk. This allows the analysis to compare risk premia across regions and over time, and to examine whether market pricing responds to changes in local economic fundamentals and policy conditions.

The broader goal is to make regional credit risk measurable. A province-level risk-premium measure can help connect local public finance to national financial stability, clarify how investors perceive implicit guarantees, and provide a more disciplined way to evaluate the distribution of risk inside China's bond market. The paper contributes to research on municipal finance, regional inequality, and the market pricing of government-related credit risk in China.
