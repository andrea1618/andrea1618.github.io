Risk-taking and Monetary Policy

Zheyu Yang · Working Paper · 2024

How does easy monetary policy change the way investors price risk? This paper studies the risk-taking channel of monetary policy in China's corporate bond market. I examine People's Bank of China announcements about reserve requirement ratios and ask whether unexpected easing makes investors more willing to hold riskier fixed-income assets.

The empirical challenge is that central bank announcements contain more than one kind of news. A policy release can change beliefs about the stance of monetary policy, but it can also reveal information about the broader economy. To separate these channels, I use high-frequency co-movement in asset prices around announcement windows and estimate a Bayesian structural vector autoregression that decomposes announcement surprises into monetary shocks and non-monetary fundamental news.

Using 16,738 corporate bonds issued by 2,711 non-financial firms from 2010 to 2020, I find that monetary shocks have large effects on bond prices, accounting for roughly one-fourth of price movements around the announcements. The effects are stronger for bonds in higher-risk groups: after an easing shock, riskier securities outperform safer ones. The response is also asymmetric, with easing having a more pronounced risk-taking effect than tightening. The results show how accommodative policy can reshape investor risk appetite and point to the importance of incorporating financial-stability considerations into monetary and macroprudential policy.
